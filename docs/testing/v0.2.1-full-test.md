# Arelis v0.2.1 — full test pass

A bottom-up walkthrough of every feature in the app, in the order that makes each
step easiest to judge. Written to be followed with the app open beside it.

You do not have to do this in one sitting. Every part stands alone, and the parts
are ordered so that nothing later depends on something earlier having worked.

---

## Before you begin

**Which copy.** This is for **dev Arelis**, launched from your dev shortcut, which
runs out of `C:\Users\you\Documents\Arelis` and keeps its data in that folder's
`data\`. The live install at `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Arelis` has its own separate
`data\`, so nothing here touches it.

**One copy at a time.** Dev and live both want port 8765 for inbound texts from
your phone. If both are running, the second one quietly moves to a different port
and your phone keeps posting to the old one. Close live Arelis before you start.

**Where to look when something is wrong.** In order of usefulness:

| Where | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| The thinking rail (`Ctrl+1`) | What she decided, which tool she reached for, how long each step took |
| `logs\arelis.log` | Everything, with stack traces |
| `logs\voice.log` | Wake decisions, what the speech model actually heard, timings |
| `logs\turns.jsonl` | One line per turn: model, tools, token counts, timings |

**Recording as you go.** For anything that goes wrong, three things make it
fixable: what you typed or said, what happened, and what you expected. A
screenshot helps but is not necessary. There's a place to collect these at the
bottom.

**A note on destructive steps.** Several steps send real email, real texts, and
create real calendar events. Each one is paired with an undo step immediately
after. Send texts to yourself, or to someone you have warned — a test message at
2am is still a message.

## Part 1 — Launch, and the first thing anyone sees

### 1.1 Cold launch

Start dev Arelis from your shortcut.

**Expect:** a dark window with a warm glow, the idle orbit in the middle, and the
words **what are we working on** under it. No docks open. No room strip.

**Look at the colour.** This is the retuned palette — it should read like an
Edison bulb or a lamp in a dark room, not like polished bronze. Shadows should be
warm red-brown rather than olive. If it still looks metallic to you, say so; this
is your eye and we tune it again.

### 1.2 The first-run dialog

Only appears when `data\first-run.json` is missing. To see it deliberately, close
Arelis, rename that file, and launch again.

**Expect:** a dark, frameless, amber-trimmed dialog titled **Welcome to Arelis**
that explains which folder she may work in, with **Choose a different folder…**
and **Start Arelis**. It should look like part of the app.

**Why this one matters:** it is the very first thing a new user ever sees. Until
tonight it rendered as a light-grey native Windows box with a standard title bar.

Restore your `first-run.json` afterwards, or just click **Start Arelis** and keep
the folder you already use.

### 1.3 The status line

Look along the top strip: the model provider, and **systems** with a count.

Click **systems**. You should get a readout of what is and is not available —
including a new **Image** entry that reports whether ComfyUI is reachable. That
entry is new tonight; previously "draw me something" would try, fail, and then
have to explain itself.

The rows are deliberately not clickable, and now say `status · read only` so
that's obvious. They used to look like menu items that ignored you.

## Part 2 — Plain conversation

### 2.1 Say hello

Type `hello` and press Enter.

**Expect:** a reply within a few seconds. First message after launch is slower
because the model loads; the thinking rail says `model loading…` while it does.

### 2.2 The suggestion chips

On a first-ever conversation, ghost chips appear under the composer offering
things like *what's the weather going to be like tomorrow?* Click one — it should
fill the composer rather than send.

### 2.3 Ask something that needs no tools

*"Explain what an interferometer measures, in two sentences."*

**Expect:** a straight answer, no tool calls in the thinking rail.

### 2.4 Stop a turn mid-flight

Ask for something long — *"write me five paragraphs about the history of optics"*
— then press **Esc** or click **stop** while she's typing.

**Expect:** she stops. The partial answer stays.

### 2.5 The role chip

Bottom-left of the composer: `fast`. Click it, choose `code`, and ask a
programming question. Watch the thinking rail confirm the model swap.

Then type `/role fast` to switch back by command. Try `/roll` — it should catch
the typo and suggest `/role`.

### 2.6 Help

Type `/help`.

**Expect:** the list of slash commands, including all five room commands, and a
`Tools:` line naming every tool currently registered. If a command works but is
missing from this list, that's a bug — one of those (`/room forget`) was found
and fixed tonight.

## Part 3 — The window itself

### 3.1 Every panel

Open each with its shortcut, confirm it appears, then close it again.

| Key | Panel | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| `Ctrl+1` | thinking | The trace from your last turn is in it |
| `Ctrl+2` | workspace | File editor, project switcher, browse list |
| `Ctrl+3` | history | Your conversations from tonight |
| `Ctrl+4` | notifications | Opens as a floating plate |
| `Ctrl+5` | camera | Preview area with start/stop |
| `Ctrl+6` | contacts | Your contact list, floating |

**What to look at while you do this:** whether they all feel like the same
application. Same corner radius, same spacing from the edge, same font, same
amber. Several of these were out of step before tonight and were fixed:

- the workspace browse list rendered filenames in a code font
- the project dropdown, the recent-files dropdown and the camera device picker
  had no styling at all and rendered as bare Windows chrome
- floating plates used a 16px corner radius, the notify card 14px, the drop
  overlay 18px, and the main window 14px, against a 12px standard — all four now
  agree
- the browser control bar's pause button was cyan, the only non-amber control in
  the app
- control heights came in three sizes (24, 28 and 34 pixels) within a single
  dock; there are now two, and they mean something: 28 for dock furniture, 34 for
  the composer
- the SMS window's minimize button wore the close button's styling, red hover
  and all

### 3.2 The view menu

Click **view** in the title bar. Every item should do something:
thinking, workspace, history, notifications, camera, contacts, always on top,
fullscreen (`F11`), shortcuts (`F1`), notify url…, reset layout.

### 3.3 Text size and layout

`Ctrl+=` a few times, then `Ctrl+-`, then `Ctrl+0` to reset. Then
**view → reset layout** and confirm you're back to conversation-only.

### 3.4 Settings

`Ctrl+,`. Walk all five tabs: **Audio**, **Window**, **Notify**, **Roots**,
**Memory**.

Check that the tabs sit at the same inset from the edge — the Memory tab used to
be tighter than the other four. Check that dropdowns and lists look like they
belong to the app rather than to Windows.

On **Audio**, use **Test mic** and **Test speak**. On **Notify**, use **Copy
primary URL** and confirm it reports what it copied.

**Note the honesty fix:** the **Listen** and **Speak** checkboxes are not fully
live — the voice service reads them when it starts. Hover each one; the tooltip
should now say it takes effect after a restart, and toggling it warns you rather
than silently doing nothing.

### 3.5 Every dialog should be dark

Three places used to open a light-grey Windows message box over the dark app.
Check each looks like part of Arelis now — frameless, amber-trimmed glass:

- deleting a conversation (history, right-click → **Delete…**)
- removing a contact (contacts plate → **remove**)
- the update prompt, if one appears at launch

On each: **Escape** should cancel, **Enter** should confirm, and the focus should
start on the *safe* answer, not the destroying one. Only the destructive button
should be red. File and folder pickers are still native Windows dialogs — that's
deliberate and not a bug.

## Part 4 — Memory, facts and history

### 4.1 Remember something

*"Remember that I climb on Tuesdays."*

**Expect:** an Allow card, because this writes. Approve it.

### 4.2 Approve the fact

Open history (`Ctrl+3`) and look at **pending facts**. Facts she extracts wait
for you there. Approve one, reject another.

### 4.3 Prove recall works

Start a new conversation (**new** in history), then ask *"when do I climb?"*

**Expect:** she recalls Tuesdays from the fact store, not from the current
conversation — which is the point.

*Requires `ollama pull nomic-embed-text` for semantic recall. Without it, facts
still store but searching them is weaker.*

### 4.4 History survives

Close Arelis completely. Reopen. Open history.

**Expect:** tonight's conversations are all still there. Nothing missing.

**Why this is a real test:** a bug fixed in this patch could delete a
conversation when two sessions started within the same second — exactly what
happens when you open and immediately close the app.

### 4.5 Delete a conversation

Right-click one in history → **Delete…**

**Expect:** a dark, in-app confirmation, not a grey Windows message box. Cancel
it, then do it again and confirm.

## Part 5 — Files and the workspace

### 5.1 Read a file

*"Read README.md and tell me what this project is."*

**Expect:** no Allow card — reading is free. The thinking rail shows the
`workspace` tool.

### 5.2 Write a file

*"Write a file called test-notes.md with three bullet points about tonight."*

**Expect:** an Allow card. Approve it. The workspace dock opens the file.

### 5.3 The projects

Type `/project` to list your folders, then `/project <name>` to switch. The
dropdown in the workspace dock should follow.

### 5.4 Browse and open

In the workspace dock: **browse**, **up**, **refresh**, and the **recent**
dropdown. Open a file by typing a path into the path field.

### 5.5 The unsaved-work guard, half one

Open a file in the dock. Type into it. **Do not save.** Now ask her to write to
that same file.

**Expect:** her write is **refused**, and a message in the chat says so. Your
typing survives.

### 5.6 The unsaved-work guard, half two

Open a file and leave it sitting. Ask her to change that file on disk. Now press
**save**.

**Expect:** first press warns you the file changed underneath. Second press
overwrites anyway. It's your file — the warning makes it a decision, not an
accident.

### 5.7 The ordinary case stays ordinary

Open a file, change a word, save. No warning, no ceremony. A guard that fires on
normal saves is worse than no guard, because you learn to ignore it.

### 5.8 A write that fails now says so

Hard to trigger deliberately, but worth knowing: if she writes a file and the
editor cannot load it back, you now get told. Before tonight the write succeeded,
the editor silently kept the old text, and nothing was said.

## Part 6 — Reading the world

### 6.1 Weather

*"What's the weather going to be like tomorrow?"*

**Expect:** a real forecast for your location, no Allow card.

Then try a city you don't live in: *"what's the weather in Reykjavik?"* This uses
a geocoding service that was blocked by a stale allowlist until this patch.

### 6.2 Location

*"Where am I?"*

### 6.3 Web search and scrape

*"Search the web for recent news about interferometry and summarise what you
find."*

**Expect:** a search followed by scrapes of the results, visible in the thinking
rail, then a summary.

### 6.4 Fetch a specific page

*"Fetch https://example.com and tell me what's on it."*

### 6.5 A research report

*"Write a thorough research report on the current state of gravitational wave
detectors."*

**Expect:** an Allow card, several minutes of work, and a markdown file in your
workspace at the end.

### 6.6 Maths

*"What is sqrt(2) times pi, to ten decimal places?"*

**Expect:** the calculator tool, and an exact answer.

Then, importantly: *"resize this to 1280 x 720 pixels"* about an image — the
calculator should **not** fire. It used to read `1280 x 720` as multiplication and
force a calculator call, which is how a thumbnail request ended in a complaint
about integrals.

## Part 7 — Seeing

### 7.1 Vision on a screenshot

Take a screenshot (`Win+Shift+S`), paste it into the composer with `Ctrl+V`, and
ask *"what's in this?"*

**Expect:** an Allow card, then an accurate description.

**Why this is the important one:** vision has **never** worked on a full-size
screenshot. A 2560x1440 image counted as more tokens than the model's whole
context window, so it was rejected before the model saw it, and the failure was
reported so politely that nobody noticed. Images are now scaled down first.

*Requires `ollama pull qwen2.5vl:3b`.*

### 7.2 Editing an image

Paste a screenshot and ask: *"make this more vibrant and resize it for a YouTube
thumbnail."*

**Expect:** your actual image, resized to 1280x720 and more saturated, in about a
second. Not a newly invented picture. Not sixty seconds of GPU time. The reply
should state the dimensions, and should say so explicitly if it had to crop.

### 7.3 Generating an image

With ComfyUI running: *"generate an image of a spiral galaxy."*

**Expect:** an Allow card, then a generated image in the workspace dock.

With ComfyUI **not** running: she should tell you it needs ComfyUI, rather than
trying and failing. Worth testing both ways — close Comfy and ask again.

### 7.4 Camera

Open the camera dock (`Ctrl+5`), **start**, then **ask Arelis**.

**Expect:** one still captured, then a vision Allow card, then a description of
what's in frame.

### 7.5 OCR

*"OCR this screenshot"* with an image containing text.

*Requires Tesseract on your PATH. If it isn't installed, she should say so
clearly.*

## Part 8 — Voice

### 8.1 The first wake is the test

Say **"Hey Arelis"** and note roughly how long it takes to respond.

**Expect:** about as fast as the fifth wake. Before this patch the first wake
after launch loaded a speech model from cold in the middle of your sentence —
tens of seconds of silence with nothing on screen. If it still lags,
`logs\voice.log` shows where the time went.

### 8.2 Wake, then speak

Say "Hey Arelis", wait for **listening**, then ask a question out loud.

### 8.3 Wake and ask in one breath

*"Hey Arelis, what's the weather tomorrow?"*

### 8.4 Dictation

`Ctrl+M`, speak, and watch the text accumulate in the composer **without**
sending. Press `Ctrl+M` again to stop.

### 8.5 Conversation mode

`Ctrl+Shift+M` for hands-free. Ask two or three things in a row. Press again to
stop.

### 8.6 Barge-in

While she's speaking, start talking.

**Expect:** she stops talking and listens. Headphones make this reliable;
speakers may cause her to hear herself.

### 8.7 What should not wake her

Say "Arelis" alone, and "Hi Arelis". Neither should wake her — the wake phrase
needs a greeting and the name. Then say something with a name-like word in the
middle of a sentence and confirm nothing happens.

## Part 9 — Email and texts

### 9.1 Read the inbox

*"Do I have any unread email?"*

**Expect:** no Allow card — reading mail is free. She lists what's there.

### 9.2 Search mail

*"Search my email for anything about interferometry."*

### 9.3 Send an email to yourself

*"Email me a test: subject 'Arelis test', body 'this is a test'."*

**Expect:** an Allow card showing recipient, subject and body **before** it
sends. Check the card is accurate, then approve. Confirm it arrives.

### 9.4 Send a text

*"Text [yourself or a warned contact]: ping from Arelis."*

**Expect:** an Allow card with the exact recipient and message. Sends never batch
— every one asks separately, every time.

### 9.5 Receive a text

Text yourself from another phone.

**Expect:** a notification pill, and the message in the notifications inbox
(`Ctrl+4`). It should **not** dump into the middle of your conversation.

### 9.6 The text conversation persists — and then doesn't

Open the text as a chat tile, reply from it, close the tile, and reopen it.

**Expect:** the conversation is still there.

Now close Arelis entirely and reopen. **Expect:** the tile starts fresh, and the
text is not in your permanent conversation history.

**Why:** this is the behaviour you chose tonight. Texts persist for the life of
the session, then start clean. There was code half-built to file them permanently
into your conversation archive; that's gone.

### 9.7 Ask about recent texts

*"Did anyone text me?"*

## Part 13 — Rooms

### 13.1 Nothing yet

`/rooms` — an explanation of what rooms are, not an empty list.

### 13.2 Make one

`/room new physics` — the strip appears above the transcript, and the transcript
is empty because you're on a new thread.

### 13.3 Configure it

- `/project` to see folder names
- `/room set purpose interferometry analysis, show your numbers`
- `/room set root <a folder from that list>`
- `/room set kind analysis`

**Expect after each:** the strip updates immediately, the workspace dock's
project switcher follows the root, and the role chip changes with the kind. All
three of those were broken or delayed at some point tonight and are now pinned by
tests.

Then get it wrong on purpose: `/room set root nonsense` should refuse **and list
the valid options**.

### 13.4 Work in it

Ask a real question about your interferometry work. Judge whether the room's
purpose is visibly shaping how she answers — this is the part no automated test
can check.

### 13.5 Leave and return

`/leave`, confirm your general conversation comes back exactly as it was, then
`/room physics` and confirm the physics thread returns and she picks up where you
left off.

### 13.6 Out loud

*"Let's work on physics."* Then *"leave the room."* Then a sentence that merely
mentions physics — nothing should move.

### 13.7 A second room

`/room new writing`, configure it differently, and step between the two. Each
keeps its own transcript, folder and model lean.

### 13.8 The strip button

Click **leave** on the strip instead of typing.

### 13.9 Let her build one

*"Make me a room for astrophysics."*

**Expect:** an Allow card, the room created — and she does **not** walk you into
it. Entering is always your move.

### 13.10 Forget one

`/room forget writing` — the room goes, the conversations stay in history. Check
history to confirm.

## Part 14 — Notifications, tray and presence

### 14.1 Notification routing

Settings → **Notify**. Set SMS to **Visual + voice**. Have someone text you while
Arelis is idle.

### 14.2 The overlay

When a notice arrives, the pill should offer **dismiss**, **snooze**, and **chat**
for texts. Try each.

### 14.3 Close to tray

With **Close to tray** on in settings, close the window.

**Expect:** it goes to the tray, and inbound texts keep arriving. Right-click the
tray icon for **Open Arelis** and **Quit Arelis**.

### 14.4 Failures now speak

Hard to force deliberately, but: if the calendar poll or the mail peek starts
failing, you should now see it said once — and see it say so again when it
recovers. Before tonight those failed silently forever.

### 14.5 No black window on launch

Watch the screen — not the app, the screen — as you double-click the Arelis
shortcut. Do it three or four times so you would catch something that only
flickers.

**Expect:** nothing appears except Arelis. No black rectangle, no console, no
flash of anything.

**If you still see one,** your existing shortcut is the old kind and will not
repair itself. Re-run `scripts\install_desktop_shortcut.ps1` from a terminal,
which rewrites both the Desktop and Start-menu shortcuts. Or fix it by hand:
right-click the shortcut → Properties, and set

- **Target:** `C:\Users\you\Documents\Arelis\.venv\Scripts\pythonw.exe -m arelis`
- **Start in:** `C:\Users\you\Documents\Arelis`
- **Run:** Normal window

The word to check for is `powershell`. If it appears in the Target, that is the
console you are seeing.

### 14.6 Clicking the shortcut while it is hidden in the tray

Close Arelis with the **X** so it goes to the tray. Then double-click the desktop
shortcut once.

**Expect:** the existing window comes back to the front, at the size and position
it was, within a second or two. No second window, no second taskbar entry, and
nothing new in Task Manager.

Then try it twice in quick succession, and again from the Start menu.

**Expect:** the same thing each time. In particular you should never have to click
it more than once — the old behaviour was to refuse silently, which is why
clicking repeatedly felt like the right thing to do.

**If it cannot reach the running copy** you should get a dark Arelis dialog saying
so and pointing at the tray icon. That is a bad outcome but a correct one; tell me
if you see it, because it means the running copy stopped answering.

### 14.7 A floating panel goes to the tray with the window

This is the one that produced the two-Arelises-on-top-of-each-other screenshot.

1. Open the **thinking** panel and drag it out of the window so it floats on its
   own.
2. Close Arelis with the **X**.

**Expect:** the floating panel disappears too. The screen should have no Arelis on
it at all.

3. Open Arelis again from the tray icon.

**Expect:** the window and the floating panel both come back, the panel in the
same place and the same size.

4. Now repeat with **minimize** instead of the X: float the panel, minimize the
window from the title bar, then restore it from the taskbar. Same expectation.

5. Finally, float the panel and choose **Quit Arelis** from the tray.

**Expect:** both windows go, and no python process is left behind.

**Why this matters:** a floating panel is a separate window as far as Windows is
concerned. One left behind on its own, with the next launch painting a fresh
window underneath it, is exactly what "two UIs stuck on top of each other" was.

### 14.8 Restoring a maximized window

Maximize Arelis, close it to the tray with the **X**, then open it again from the
tray icon.

**Expect:** it comes back maximized, in one step. You should not see a full-screen
copy appear and then shrink, and you should not see two frames at once.

Then quit while maximized, and launch again.

**Expect:** it opens. This is worth checking on its own — a maximized window that
had been saved and restored was, until tonight, capable of failing to start at all
with "Arelis window failed to start".

## Part 15 — Shutting down and coming back

### 15.1 Quit properly

Quit from the tray. Confirm the process actually exits.

**Expect:** Quit still feels instant — it should not pause, spin, or hang for a
second while it tidies up.

Then open `logs\arelis.log` and look at the last few lines.

**Expect:** no `Task was destroyed but it is pending`, and no `Indexed N workspace
file(s) into memory` arriving *after* the quit line. Both of those meant the app
was being switched off with a database write still in progress.

### 15.1a How many python processes is normal

Open Task Manager while Arelis is running and count the `python` entries.

**Expect: two.** This is normal and is not a leak. Python on this machine is
installed through the new install manager, where the thing you launch is a small
redirector that starts the real interpreter as a child and then just waits. The
parent uses about 3 MB and does nothing; the child is the actual Arelis. Every
Python program on the machine does this, including `python -c "print(1)"`.

**What would be a real problem:** the count growing over time, or processes left
behind after Quit. After a tray Quit the count should return to zero.

### 15.2 Relaunch and check what survived

| Should survive | Should not |
|---|---|
| Conversations in history | Which room you were in |
| Rooms and their configuration | Open text conversations |
| Approved facts | Window layout, unless you saved it |
| Contacts, tasks, goals | |

### 15.3 The layout comes back

Before quitting, open two docks and drag one out to float it. Quit, relaunch.

**Expect:** the docks return where you left them, including the floating one.

**Why this is worth a step:** the four docks carry object names that no
stylesheet uses, which made them look like dead code tonight. They are what Qt
matches saved layouts against — deleting them would have broken exactly this,
and it would not have shown up for days.

### 15.4 The CLI

From a terminal: `arelis --cli`. Say hello, run `/help`, then `/exit`.

## What to send back

For each problem: **what you did**, **what happened**, **what you expected**.

Group them however is easiest. If you'd rather just say "part 7.3 is broken and
here's the screenshot", that works too.

Three specific opinions I'd like beyond pass/fail:

1. **The colour.** Does it read as lamplight now, or still as bronze? Where
   specifically, if anywhere, does it still feel off?
2. **The rooms.** Does working inside one actually feel different from the general
   conversation, or does it feel like a label on the same thing?
3. **The Allow cards.** Too many, too few, or about right? Is there anything she
   asks permission for that she shouldn't have to, or anything she does without
   asking that made you uneasy?
